P2P energy trading · study design

Networks, prosumer populations and trading graphs

The study runs one set of prosumers on four distribution feeders, at four population sizes and with three trading graphs. That makes 48 structures, each with its own results folder and its own 36 analysis days. Everything is selected in config.py.

The four feeders

IEEE 33-bus

pandapower case33bw at 12.66 kV, the original reference. It is one radial feeder with three laterals. Every line is 1 km long in the pandapower data, so kNN distances on IEEE33 count lines rather than real lengths. The 5 MVA line limit is a placeholder and never binds.

SimBench LV feeders

Rural, semi-urban and urban 0.4 kV grids (1-LV-rural3, 1-LV-semiurb5, 1-LV-urban6, no switches). The 20/0.4 kV transformer is not modelled: the LV busbar is the root, held at 1.0 p.u. like the IEEE33 root. Line limits use the real cable rating, √3 · 0.4 kV · Imax. Two semi-urban buses allow only 1.01 p.u. at most.

All feeders keep their static base loads, as the IEEE33 model always did. Prosumer demand comes on top of the base load at the same bus.

Prosumer populations

Every network uses exactly the same prosumers as IEEE33 for each size; only their buses differ. Populations are nested, 4 ⊂ 8 ⊂ 16: when N grows, the existing prosumers stay where they are and new ones are added.

  • The office building (G4-B, 30 kWp PV, workplace EV) belongs to N = 4, 8 and 16. The N = 32 set does not include it, so N = 32 is not a superset of N = 16. Its place in the original IEEE33 design (bus 11) is taken by an EV-only household.
  • Heat-pump profiles are drawn per run with seed 42 from the Hanover/Lübeck SimBench heat-pump profiles. EVs use SimBench home (HLS) or workplace (APLS) charging profiles, with a fallback schedule on days without EV activity.
  • Demand profiles: H0 residential, G commercial; PV profiles: SimBench PV1–PV8 scaled to the listed kWp.

Prosumers in the selected structure

Where the prosumers sit

IEEE33 keeps its original bus assignment. For the three SimBench feeders, each IEEE33 location was mapped to a distinct LV load bus by one random permutation per network. The permutation was drawn once on 2026-11-26 and is hardcoded in populations/simbench_lv*.py as original pandapower bus ids, so nothing is random at run time. Neighbours in IEEE33 are generally not neighbours in the LV feeders.

Trading graphs

Cyclic

A ring over the prosumer order: 1–2, 2–3, …, N–1. Every prosumer has exactly two trading partners, whatever the feeder.

kNN (k = 3)

Each prosumer links to its three nearest prosumers by distance along the feeder (km of cable), and links are made mutual. Components are joined by their closest pair until the graph is connected. Degree is therefore at least 3 and can be higher for central prosumers.

Sparse heterogeneous

A preferential-attachment tree (each new prosumer links to an existing one with probability ∝ degree), then extra links up to a mean degree of 2.5, again favouring well-connected prosumers. This gives a few trading hubs and many prosumers with one or two partners. The graph depends only on N and the seed (42), not on the feeder.

Folder names

<network>_<N>_<graph>, with graph tags cyclic, knn3 and sparsehet2.5s42. Existing IEEE33 cyclic folders keep their names.

Analysis days

The base days are the 7th, 14th and 21st of every month of 2016 (36 days, each with a 24 h lookahead). Each network shifts them by its own offset, so the study covers 144 distinct days in total. IEEE33 keeps the original days, so existing results stay valid.

Feasibility check

The original centralized solver (algo_central.centralized, 30 min, 48 h horizon) was run on the largest population in each feeder, for a winter and a summer day, and evaluated with the original feeder sweep. All runs are feasible with headroom. The tighter LV cable ratings do not bind at N = 32.

Total cost is the same across feeders on the same day because no network limit binds. It differs by day because prices and profiles differ. These checks used day 2016-01-07/2016-07-07 for every network, before the per-network date offsets were added.
